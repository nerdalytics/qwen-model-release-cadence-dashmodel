Methodology

How the filters and estimates work.

Separate model histories

Max, Plus, Flash, parameter sizes and other types are calculated separately. A batch belongs to each type named in its source evidence. Coding sizes have separate histories from core sizes. Generation counts each numbered generation once at its first public launch, through an API or open weights. This includes 3.5, 3.6, 3.7 and 3.8. Later sizes and tiers do not count as another generation.

Average and EMA

Historical mean uses all gaps between distinct dates for one type. Recent mean and median use the last three gaps. Weighted mean gives the last three gaps 20%, 30% and 50% weight, renormalized when fewer are available. EMA starts with the first gap, then gives each new gap 20% weight and the previous EMA 80%. Each estimate adds that interval to the latest entry for the same type. EMA smooths historical gaps. It does not project a shortening trend, and the initial gap can dominate when there are few intervals.

Trend methods

Holt tracks interval level and trend with α = 0.50 and β = 0.30. Damped Holt applies φ = 0.80 in its updates and projection. Linear interval trend fits gap length against gap index. At least three gaps are required. Non-positive predictions show no date instead of a clamped result.

Probability models

Lognormal renewal fits the logarithms of at least three gaps. Its point estimate is the median. Bayesian partial pooling assumes exponential waiting times with a Gamma rate prior. Prior shape is 2; its rate is the related peer types’ mean gap. This is an empirical-Bayes hierarchy with fixed prior strength, not a fully fitted hierarchy. It needs at least three distinct peer intervals and one own gap. Generation has no peer cohort and shows no pooled estimate.

Hosted peers are Max, Plus, Flash and Turbo. Coding peers are other Coder types. Other core types use other non-generation core variants, excluding context-only updates. Own-type events are excluded from peer histories; matching peer date pairs count once. Pooling assumes related types have comparable rates. That assumption may be wrong.

Windows and elapsed time

Probability windows span the fitted 10th to 90th percentiles. They are not calibrated confidence bounds and can be wide. Lognormal windows exclude parameter uncertainty; Bayesian windows include uncertainty in the type’s rate but not the peer-prior fit. The optional no-newer-release assumption conditions those two models on the elapsed wait. It never moves the other forecasts forward.

Historical checks

Walk-forward tests forecast each recorded event using only earlier events. Peer histories are also cut off at that time. Mean absolute error is in days and covers only valid point forecasts. Unavailable predictions are counted separately, so rejection-prone methods may appear better than they are. Fewer than three valid tests is too little for ranking. Historical tests are unconditioned even when today’s conditional assumption is enabled. They do not establish a winning method.

Filters and updates

Date range changes the graph and log, not the forecast history. Core and Coding restrict the available types. New generations, sizes and tiers are releases. Include updates adds changes to existing models to each type’s history. Same-day entries count once per type. All types shows combined history without a model forecast.

Limited and past estimates

At least two dated entries are needed. Simple methods flag fewer than three intervals as limited history. Trend and lognormal methods flag fewer than six. Bayesian pooling also needs enough peer history. A past estimate remains in the past. We do not restart the clock from today or invent missed releases.

These dates are estimates, not announcements. A past estimate does not prove a release was missed. The dataset may lack newer events. Sources are checked manually. This is not a live feed.
